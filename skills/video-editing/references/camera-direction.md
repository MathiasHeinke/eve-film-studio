# Choose the moment and camera treatment

Load this guide when deciding where an alternate angle, aerial view or B-roll
belongs. It supports the existing cut plan; it does not create another agent,
project, execution ledger or mandatory approval sequence.

## Proactive use is the default

On a creative editing request, use this catalog without waiting for the user
to ask about camera angles. Look for a few high-value opportunities in the
actual material and explain them through the moment and intended effect, not
through a list of technical capabilities. A singing phrase, an emotional
reaction, a reveal or a practical action can each motivate a different shot.

Make concrete choices within the user's brief and existing product authority.
If generation is already covered by the task's budget/permissions, prepare and
execute the selected cut through the normal workflow. Otherwise prepare the
exact shot, timing and price before seeking the missing authorization. Do not
turn every cut into a new approval dialog or make the user design the edit.
Avoid adding effects merely to demonstrate AI; preserving a strong original
moment can be the best editorial choice.

## Start with the scene's purpose

Watch and listen before choosing a shot. Find the action or musical/emotional
beat the user wants to emphasize. If they mention a singing passage, locate
that passage in the actual sound and footage and mark its source time. A smiling
frame elsewhere or an easily hidden camera wobble is not an equivalent choice.
Existing transcripts, waveform/beat analysis and markers can help, but do not
infer singing, a lyric or its timecode from a silent thumbnail.

For an open brief, propose or choose the strongest relevant moment and give a
short reason tied to what is actually visible/audible. Reuse the user's selected
moment when provided. Ask only when the intended scene remains ambiguous.

## Make the angle serve that moment

| Intended effect | Candidate treatment | What to preserve/check |
|---|---|---|
| Emotion or a sung/spoken phrase | Eye-level close or medium shot, modest side angle | Expression, performer, mouth/gesture timing and original sound |
| A relationship or group interaction | Wider three-quarter angle or reaction view | Participant positions, gaze and the same timed interaction |
| A place reveal or musical lift | Oblique overhead, elevated wide or drone-style view | Group remains locatable; spatial orientation, light and reveal timing make sense |
| A specific object/action | Detail or insert shot | Correct object state, hand/action continuity and narrative relevance |
| Illustrate dialogue or cover a join | Relevant B-roll | Visual meaning, length and cut boundaries; do not label it a synchronous second camera |

These are choices, not defaults. Do not add an orbit, drone rise or slow motion
merely because the model can do it. One well-motivated angle can be sufficient.
For a two-second slot, starting already in an elevated view is often clearer
than spending the entire slot travelling there. Use a longer reveal only when
the actual edit has room for it.

## Perspective and movement catalog

| Technique | Useful situations / intent | Selection check |
|---|---|---|
| Close-up / tighter detail | A lyric, emotional reaction, hands doing meaningful work | Keep the decisive expression or action readable |
| Three-quarter alternate angle | Conversation, group singing, demonstrations | Same moment, participant positions and plausible sight lines |
| Over-the-shoulder / reverse | Dialogue, teaching, someone showing an object | Maintain who looks at whom and what is being shown |
| Point-of-view | A discovery, opening something, moving through a place | It must match whose viewpoint the story follows |
| Low angle | A performance, a large object, a deliberate sense of scale | Avoid accidental body/product distortion |
| Elevated wide / oblique overhead | Group performance, travel, a location reveal | Subjects remain locatable and orientation stays understandable |
| Top-down | Cooking, craft, tabletop products, spatial arrangement | Hands, objects and their layout remain coherent |
| Drone-style rise / pull-away | Musical lift, finale, reveal of the surrounding place | Needs time for the reveal; short slots may start already elevated |
| Lateral tracking / parallel move | Walking, sport, a moving subject | Preserve speed, action direction and continuity |
| Restrained orbit / arc | Sculpture, product, a spatial relationship | Reveals genuinely useful sides; does not change the action or object |
| Push-in / pull-back | Build attention or release into wider context | Match the emotional or musical beat and available duration |
| Foreground reveal / parallax | Architecture, an entrance, a scene transition | Foreground/background layers must form a plausible space |
| Focus shift | Transfer attention between a person and an object | A focus decision, not a replacement for missing source information |
| Reaction / environmental B-roll | Cover a join, illustrate speech, add atmosphere | Must have a reason at this exact point and the correct duration |

Combine sparingly. For example, a short singing insert may need one stable
elevated wide, while a longer musical ending can support a gradual pull-away.
A close-up of hands can improve a tutorial more than an aerial spectacle.

## Fill a real capability gap without being prompted

If the intended moment needs a technique this catalog or connected tools do
not cover, first inspect existing specialized skills, the editor's native
features and current provider documentation. `community-sources.md` identifies
source-reviewed starting points for this search. Perform focused research for the
missing operation or a maintained workflow instead of asking the user to find
the tool. Check actual reference modalities, input restrictions, price and
version; a public demo is a candidate, not proof in this environment.

Reuse a suitable existing capability. New software, credentials or paid calls
still follow the current task authority; research does not grant extra scope.
Use the existing project notes and native skill-improvement path to retain a
technique that worked, including its prerequisites and limitations. Mark an
untried idea as a candidate. Do not copy private client footage, transcripts or
preferences into the shared shipped catalog, or start an unrelated background
research/installation loop.

## Describe a camera, not just “cinematic”

Bind the shot brief to the existing source/timeline cut plan. Specify:

- Subject and the timed action that must remain the same.
- Shot size and viewpoint relative to the scene: e.g. elevated three-quarter
  wide, looking diagonally down at the group, rather than an unspecified drone.
- Camera height/distance or other spatial anchors when they can be meaningfully
  estimated; avoid contradictory or falsely measured geometry.
- Whether the camera is fixed or moves, its initial/final composition and how
  that movement fits the insert's duration. A new viewpoint is not necessarily
  a camera move from the old viewpoint.
- Incoming/outgoing action, gaze, screen direction, light and background anchors.
- Exact usable duration, handles and output-to-timeline mapping from the cut plan.

For a singing moment, keep the original performance and musical phrase timing.
If mouths are visible, inspect synchronization with the actual syllables; a
similar smiling pose is not enough. An elevated view may instead emphasize the
group and place, but its action still belongs to the selected moment. New aerial
geometry is synthesized where no reference shows it, not recovered ground truth.

## Connected generated scenes

For a chain of generated narrative shots, use the shared
[continuity and staged review method](../../produce-brand-film/references/continuity-and-review.md).
Choose the next angle from the accepted predecessor's actual action/eyeline
state. Identity references and planned poses alone do not establish a match.
Review the real join before continuing that dependent chain; independent
shots can still be produced in parallel.

## Generate, judge and insert against the brief

Use the selected moment and adjacent context as references. Preserve participant
appearance, clothing and scene anchors explicitly in the prompt. Follow
`generated-inserts.md` for provider selection, duration, input handling and cost.
Do not choose the destination of a generic generated shot only after it returns.

Judge the full proposed insert in context with its lead-in and follow-through.
Check the chosen viewpoint, subject visibility, orientation, action/music timing,
likeness, anatomy and camera motion. Reject a wrong moment even if the image is
beautiful. Keep original sound and media; place accepted material at the exact
planned time marks through the editor's normal recoverable edit path.

## Example decision, not an unrun capability claim

If the user asks to lift a singing scene with a drone perspective: locate the
actual phrase first, select its exact insert window, then choose an elevated
composition that reveals the place while keeping the singers readable. Generate
for that timed window and judge the musical/action continuity. The completed H3
pilot demonstrated a new viewpoint and a two-second insertion; it did not test
a singing-synchronized aerial shot.
