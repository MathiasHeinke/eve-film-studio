# Narration production

Read when the brief requires a narrator or spoken performance. This adds a
voice-production path; [film craft](film-craft.md) owns sound direction and
[review gates](review-gates.md#sound--only-with-qualified-hearing) own listening.

## Write for the film, not over it

Give spoken lines a narrative job: establish the problem, connect a causal turn,
clarify product contribution or carry an emotional change. Leave useful silence
for action, music and recognition. Readability of a caption is not a reason to
repeat it verbatim in voice-over. Maintain truthful product/permission claims.

Write the requested languages deliberately, preserving meaning and natural
phrasing rather than mechanically matching word count. Keep complete phrases,
natural pauses and pronunciation notes in a script linked from the storyboard.
Let delivered speech influence timing; do not squeeze every take into a guessed
window or unnaturally speed it up to preserve an early cut.

## Prove a voice with a short passage

Choose a bounded sample that represents the desired delivery, including an
important name or tricky phrase when relevant. Use a small number of plausible
catalogue voices/performers before synthesising every line in every alternative.
The user may grant the artist voice choice; do not invent an extra approval
requirement or a fixed male/female narrator preference.

Verify the current route, voice identifier, input support, licence and budget.
Request only authorized generation; no silent provider/model/account fallback.
Keep original audio and per-take provenance. Voice cloning or a specific real
person's imitation is a separate choice with its applicable permission boundary.

The character/byte/token/second pricing unit must be verified, not inferred from
a numeric rate alone. If a binary audio response supplies a generation id,
read its billing receipt; a temporarily missing receipt is unknown, not free.
Preserve its reservation and do not duplicate an uncertain paid request blindly.
Provider APIs and prices change; do not freeze model/voice ids in this workflow.

## Produce and inspect the actual take

Probe format, duration, sample rate and channels, then check the complete spoken
passage. Transcription can reveal missing words or pronunciation failures;
it does not certify warmth, natural prosody, pacing, intelligibility or musical
quality. Do not fragment speech into separate line calls unless the cut benefits
and consistency/intonation have been checked.

When no qualified hearing is available, state that precisely. Arrange a capable
audio/audiovisual reviewer or an explicit user listening handoff; do not call
waveform analysis a listening test. A model's ingestion receipts establish input
delivery, not guaranteed perceptual accuracy.

Keep voice, music and ambience/effects separate in the source. Place them on the
film's common timeline, protect quiet word boundaries, and leave speech room in
the mix. Use measured loudness/peaks to support the mix, then listen to the exact
exported result. Preserve original takes when trimming, ducking or normalising.

For audible on-screen dialogue, review the whole phrase and adjacent shots for
lip/action sync. Voice-over needs coherent story timing without implying a lip
sync test occurred. Recheck the requested language versions after actual mixing.

Record selected voice/takes, source rights, script-to-timeline ranges, confirmed
and unresolved costs, listening scope and outstanding checks in the existing
production/provenance and review owners. Deliver a playable voice sample or
narrated passage before calling the spoken film ready.
