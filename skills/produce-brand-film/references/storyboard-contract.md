# Storyboard Contract

Before a dependent generation batch, settle the story, required actions and
continuity. Planned times and handles are production targets; record the actual
source-to-timeline mapping after inspecting the delivered performance.

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| id | Stable shot id such as S03. |
| time | Planned in/out, duration and handles; actual selected range recorded after inspection. |
| narrative_job | What the viewer learns or feels through this shot and what observable change carries it. |
| visual | Subject, action, framing, camera movement, environment. |
| continuity | Required identity, wardrobe, props, light and scene relationships. |
| entry_exit_state | Relevant gaze target/height, body and hands, held objects, action phase at both boundaries. |
| predecessor | Accepted prior shot/frame/interval if this shot depends on its ending; otherwise independent. |
| source_route | Real footage, UI capture, image/video reference generation, or motion graphic. |
| exact_overlay | Required copy or none; choose and verify the rendering route separately. |
| graphic_function | Input composer, assistant message, artifact/status, editorial context, brand statement, or none. |
| audio | Speaker, complete phrase, music, ambience or silence; visual and audio source ranges kept separately. |
| transition | What motivates the join; what is continuous and what change or time ellipse the viewer can infer. |
| fallback | Smallest story-consistent replacement if the action or continuity fails. |
| claim_class | Source-backed, product-truth, opinion, aspirational, illustrative example, or blocked. |

For connected scenes, use [continuity-and-review.md](continuity-and-review.md).
Review finished storyboard, ordered anchors and actual adjacent clips at their
respective stages. These fields aid judgment; filling them is not a PASS.

## Pacing starting points

Establishing shots may need2–4s; brief reaction/inserts may need less; emotional
recognition and an ending need room. Choose time from the actual action,
spoken phrase and required reading, not a fixed template. One clear message
at a time; verify at the intended viewing size.

## Character continuity

Give every recurring person a concise card: appearance, wardrobe, relevant
props, scene position, camera side and approved identity reference. Use only
as many characters and returns as the story needs. Keep actual shot-state
references separate from identity portraits. Use fictional people unless the
user supplied consented real-person assets; no invented customer testimony.
