# Character and scene look development

Read when a film depends on recurring people/mascots or its character look is
being revised. [Story continuity](continuity-and-review.md) owns action and
joins; this reference establishes a convincing visual identity before a batch.

## Settle the look with actual studies

Translate the brief/reference into an intentional visual language: illustration,
sculpted animation, stop motion, stylised 3D, realism or another fitting medium.
Keep appearance, light, materials and acting coherent. Do not assume realistic
human proportions improve an otherwise toy-like design.

Make a small, useful set of studies before the dependent film render: a readable
face/expression, body/pose and at least one representative environmental view.
The exact count depends on uncertainty. Inspect faces at close range and the
whole scene at delivery size. More geometry is useful only if the result improves.

For generated references, distinguish identity, style, composition and edit
targets. Use the selected image skill/authorized provider, retain real output
and provenance, and verify supported reference inputs. A character sheet is
not proof that separate video generations will keep that person consistent.

## Examine the failure modes directly

- **Scale:** compare people with the doors, floors, desks, chairs and props in
  the same scene. Use a consistent world scale or ratio scheme. Stylisation may
  exaggerate proportions intentionally; it should not accidentally change
  between shots. Check standing, sitting and the essential interaction.
- **Face and expression:** inspect eyes/gaze, mouth, brows, hair silhouette,
  recognition and emotional readability. Avoid adding isolated realistic eyes
  to a primitive rigid face as a supposed quality upgrade.
- **Body and action:** inspect shoulder/hip relationships, joints, hands,
  contact, weight and pose. A rigid mannequin with extra textures may still
  fail the required appeal and performance.
- **Materials and light:** judge actual skin/fabric/hair/prop surfaces and
  shadow/contact behaviour. Texture names and renderer features are not evidence
  of believable craft.
- **Continuity:** record a concise identity card for hair, wardrobe, palette and
  relevant props; test recognition in the important recurring angles.

When the user calls the result uncanny, primitive or inconsistent, identify
the visible cause and redesign it. Repeatedly changing height, antialiasing or
colour grade does not resolve a character-art problem. Consider another medium
within the user's constraints if the current method cannot produce the desired
look. Do not treat one campaign's rejected LEGO/toy style as a universal ban.

## Prove performance before volume

Test the difficult connected action in a short moving passage: for example a
handoff, reacting to an agent or handling a small object. Include relevant
neighbouring states. A pleasing portrait alone does not establish usable hands,
acting, environment fit or continuity. Exact UI, labels and logos should retain
their verified native assets and be inspected in the composed shot.

Keep chosen identity/scene studies and rejected versions in the current
production folder. Record the design choice and observed limits in the existing
storyboard/review records. Progress within existing authorization; ask for user
input when a material taste decision cannot be resolved from the brief or when
the user explicitly wants to choose. Keep creative acceptance separate from an
author's own readiness report.
