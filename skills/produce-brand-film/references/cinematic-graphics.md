# Cinematic titles and product proof

Use for brand films where messages, documents, calendars or end titles share
the screen with photographed people and places. It is an art-direction method,
not a new renderer, tool registry or permission system.

## Choose the information's role before its visual treatment

For every overlay, answer: who is communicating, what just happened, what the
viewer needs to understand, and whether this is part of the interaction or an
editorial comment. Choose a visual grammar that makes that role recognisable.
A beautiful lower third does not automatically read as an assistant message.

| What the scene needs to reveal | Suitable grammar | What makes it understandable |
|---|---|---|
| The person composing a request | Floating composer, text field or faithful device UI | Editable text/caret and a clear send or submit moment when relevant. Synchronise with actual typing; voice input need not pretend to be keystrokes. |
| A user's sent message or an assistant reply | Familiar chat/message bubble, comparable in clarity to messaging apps | Sender and direction are unambiguous; entry follows the relevant action, response or turn. The message may settle for reading while the person remains in motion. |
| A completed file or delivered result | Attachment, document preview or result inside the assistant's output | The object and its completion/dispatch state remain associated. Distinguish a promise, a draft and a completed action. |
| Background progress that matters to the story | Restrained task/status element | Clarify whose process it is and which change is relevant; do not invent measured progress or timing. |
| Name, role, place or editorial context | Lower third or contextual caption | It reads as information supplied by the film, without misleading input/send affordances. |
| Campaign statement or brand closure | Free typography and exact brand artwork | It reads as the film's statement; preserve human performance and a readable final hold. |

Choose among these according to the moment; none is a universal default or ban.
They can coexist within one film when their different jobs stay clear. A
messaging-app analogy supplies familiar interaction language, not a requirement
to copy another product's logo, palette or actual UI. Preserve accurate product
artwork when claiming to show the real interface; identify staged examples.

## Design the chosen grammar inside the scene

Plan graphics beside the performance and camera. A floating composer or reply
can make otherwise invisible information legible without cutting to a monitor.
Keep the live scene's human action active; place the element where attention can
move between the person and the needed words. A physical-screen insert is
another valid choice, not a mandatory containment rule.

Define trigger, entrance, readable hold, state change and exit. Decide whether
the element belongs to the screen plane, world space or film's graphic layer.
Do not accidentally attach it to a moving face or prop, or let it drift like
an untracked object. Check exact wording, speaker identity, hierarchy and
reading time at the intended delivery size. Remove information the performance
already communicates unless repetition has a deliberate purpose.

Compare the human frame, interaction/result frame and ending together. Match
appropriate light, depth, palette and typography without making every type of
information look identical. "Cinematic" describes the integration and effect;
it does not select a specific panel shape, material or text animation.

## Choose who renders the letters

- Use precise native/editor/SVG typography for critical values, complete
  documents, legal text, URLs and approved brand artwork.
- Generated lettering can be useful for a short expressive line or a physical
  sign when the requested model supports it. If the user wants this route,
  test one scoped shot with the exact wording before generating many variants.
- A practical hybrid is generated or photographed motion underneath exact
  typography. Precise letters do not require a flat background or a static card.
- Keep original logos and required provenance marks. A generated word in a
  type experiment is not automatically the approved logo.

For a generative composer/message test, specify its communication role and
required state transitions as well as wording, hierarchy, contrast, entrance,
readable hold and exit. H3 may render that element when supported and verified;
precise compositing is also valid. Judge the scene, not the provider name. Inspect the full intended insertion interval: accents, letter
shapes, numbers and spacing may drift between otherwise attractive frames.
Choose the result by the film's visual and comprehension needs, not by which
rendering method produced it.

## Direct the typography

Set font family, weight, size, line breaks, safe area and motion deliberately.
Variable fonts need explicit axis/weight values: their default can be an
unexpectedly thin instance. Check the actual rendered font, not only its name.

Tie movement to a meaningful event: a request arrives, a document settles into
view, a reserved time appears, or a brand line resolves over the human payoff.
Avoid animating every letter simply to show motion. Allow enough settled time
to read the actual message at the intended viewing size.

Proof inserts can use a real artifact, a tightly framed application detail or
an expressly staged example. They need recognizable information hierarchy and
the same visual care as the people. A staged result must not masquerade as a
verified product run or measured performance.

## Validate and retain the useful result

Judge entry/middle/exit in sequence and beside adjacent footage. Check contrast,
font weight, temporal spelling, crop safety, face clearance and whether the
result is understandable muted. Automated checks do not replace a human
comprehension test; label unrun tests accurately.

Preserve chosen style frames, exact copy, renderer/font settings and source
references in the existing production folder. Reuse the existing film skill,
timeline, native editor and media pipeline. Extend a helper only for a concrete
repeated gap; do not create a separate typography agent framework or ledger.
